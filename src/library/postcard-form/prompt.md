Build a React + TypeScript + Tailwind "Postcard Form": a contact form laid out as a postcard, where the stamp in the corner is the submit button. It fixes three real problems with contact forms: an accidental refresh or tab close throws the message away, sending while offline fails silently, and errors only show up as a wall of red after you press send. No extra libraries.

Props (typed): onSend (values: { message, name, email, topic }) => Promise<void> (resolve when delivered, reject to bounce), draftKey (localStorage key, default "postcard-form"), topics?: string[] (shows an optional topic select when given), minMessageLength (default 10), maxLength (default 1000), labels (partial overrides for every visible and announced string: title, to, from, field labels and placeholders, stamp, stampBusy, stampArt, stampValue, postmark word, date and "ago" formatters, restored, discard, saved, counter, nearLimit, atLimit, each error, invalid summary, sending, mailbox, takeBack, error, tryAgain, sent, sentNote, writeAnother), accent (stamp and postmark ink, default "#3DD9FF"), size ("sm" | "md" | "lg"), disabled, online (force the network state; by default it follows navigator.onLine and the online/offline events), previewState ("empty" | "filled" | "invalid" | "sending" | "mailbox" | "sent" | "error", drawn still without storage or timers), callbacks onDraftSave, onDraftRestore(values, savedAt), onInvalid(fields), onQueue(values), onError(error), and className.

Behaviour:
- Draft autosave: 400 ms after the last keystroke the values are written to localStorage under draftKey with a timestamp (removed when the card is empty). Storage is only read after mount, in a timer, so the server and client HTML always match. A restored draft shows "Restored your draft from 2 min ago · Discard". The draft is kept while the card waits in the mailbox and cleared only after a successful send.
- Validation: name required, email required and well formed, message at least minMessageLength characters. A field is checked when you leave it (empty untouched fields are not flagged yet) and re-checked as you type once it has an error. Errors sit under their field, linked with aria-describedby and aria-invalid. On send, every field is checked, focus moves to the first invalid one, the status line says how many things to fix, and the stamp shakes.
- Character counter "n / max" under the message (textarea maxLength enforces the cap). It turns amber at 90% and a status region announces once near the limit and once at it, not every keystroke.
- Send online: the stamp sticks on, the postmark inks over it with today's date, aria-busy is set and fields go read-only while onSend runs. Success: the card slides up into a brass letterbox slot and the panel says "Posted ✓" with the postmark and a "Write another" button, which gets focus.
- Send offline: the stamp sticks on and the card says "Waiting in the mailbox · sends when you're back online" with a "Take it back" button. When the network returns it sends by itself, exactly once (an in-flight guard stops the online event and a click both sending).
- Failure: the card stays with every word kept, a role="alert" line says it didn't go through, and "Try again" gets focus.
- Real <form> with <label>s and native inputs: Enter in a single-line field sends, Ctrl/Cmd+Enter sends from the message.

Look: a cream paper card (#F7F2E5) with a soft shadow on any stage. Left half is the message on blue-ruled lined paper with a serif italic title; right half is the address side with dashed underline fields, a printed "To:" line and a "From" heading, split by a thin rule. Under a 560 px container width (container query) the halves stack, message on top, and the stamp stays pinned top-right. The empty stamp box is dashed; hover lifts and tilts it, press shrinks it. The stuck stamp has perforated edges, the accent colour with readable text picked by luminance, a value and a little picture. The postmark is a double ink ring with the word arched inside, the date in the middle and wavy cancellation lines across the stamp, in a darkened mix of the accent. Ink on paper stays dark for 4.5:1 contrast, the focus ring on the card is a dark postal blue so it shows on paper on both light and dark stages, and reduced motion turns off the stick, ink, shake and slide.
